# ADR-0207 · 2026-11-25 · A stroll or dog walk is a loop and stays where it starts, unless it goes out from home and comes straight back: the secondary location stage places a loop activity at the place of the activity before it and keeps a destination for an out-and-back stroll

- **Status:** active on branch `feature/i679-stroll-loops` (not merged; `active` in the sense of docs/decisions/README.md applies once on `main`). Implemented; 1 % evidence recorded in the run manifest [`docs/runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml`](../runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml) (a wiring proof and an A/B of model output, no observed reference compared; arms A to D ran before the out-and-back change of 2026-11-26; arm E, the run with it, is recorded in the same manifest, metrics "arm E against arm D - ..."); owner assessment pending.
- **Numbering:** ADR-0207 is the next free id after ADR-0206 (`fix/i678-mode-choice-reach`, same-day parallel branch); checked on 2026-11-25 across all refs (`git for-each-ref` and `git ls-tree` of `docs/decisions/`; the highest id taken is ADR-0206).
- **Issue:** #679. **Owner decision (2026-11-25):** follow the eqasim-france Paris model, a loop gets (almost) no displacement; keep sampling the day from one diary and the secondary distances by class as eqasim does, with no cap on drawn distances and no bound from the diary's own distance; try to identify loops also where the detailed purpose was not asked; one plan with #678. **Owner decision (2026-11-26):** a stroll that goes out from home and whose next diary trip goes home is not a loop; it keeps a destination, and the trip into it stays on walk or bicycle (decisions 1, 2 and 6 as refined below).
- **Rule and definition:** `docs/codebase/notes/leisure-loops.md` (the one home of the definitions of stroll, loop and out-and-back, the placement rule, the marker and the two mode rules). **Switch:** feature record `secondary_loop_zero_displacement`.

## Context

- MiD records a stroll (`W_ZWD` 710) or a dog walk (711) as ONE trip whose length is the whole loop, except that a part of them is followed by a way home or back whose length is about that of the way out (for that subset the diary looks like out-and-back, which decision 1 uses); the shares and the length ratio are the metric "how strolls (710), dog walks (711) and active sport (704) are recorded, by mode" of the survey manifest below. The model divides that path length by the detour factor and uses it as the distance between two places, so a stroll connects two far-apart activities and the next trip starts from a place the person never went to. How the survey records these trips, how many long walk and bicycle trips they are and how well the diary chain alone detects them: [`docs/runs/i679-stroll-loops-survey-probe-2026-11-25.yml`](../runs/i679-stroll-loops-survey-probe-2026-11-25.yml).
- **What the French eqasim pipeline does** (read in `eqasim-org/ile-de-france` at commit `7d219a07`, 2026-11-26): it has no stroll rule and no stroll activity type. The EGT purpose "loisirs" (`data/hts/egt/cleaned.py::PURPOSE_MAP`, code 8) and the ENTD purposes 5, 7 and 8 (`data/hts/entd/cleaned.py::PURPOSE_MAP`) all become `leisure`. A stroll is therefore an ordinary leisure activity at a leisure location, placed by the secondary location solver like any other (`synthesis/population/spatial/secondary/locations.py`), and the way home starts from that location. The distance it targets is drawn per mode and travel-time band (`synthesis/population/spatial/secondary/distance_distributions.py`) from the EGT field `DPORTEE`, which the pipeline reads as `euclidean_distance`, with loop trips kept in the pool; the walk distributions are additionally shifted towards shorter distances (`resample_distributions`, factor -0.5 for walk). The pool of a mode and band is shared by the non-primary legs of every purpose, so the near-zero loop distances shorten the draws of all of them, and a stroll draws from that mixed pool like any other leg: nothing places it near its start, it lands there only as often as the pool gives a short distance. This model already sampled the same way before this change (the same bands and trip filter, `braunschweig/popsim/distance_distributions.py`, and the same resampling factors, `secondary_chainsolvers._build_shared_solve_state`), with two differences that predate it: the pool is split by purpose (`secondary_distance_by_purpose`), and its distances are MiD path lengths divided by the detour factor, which for a loop is the whole loop instead of a near-zero straight line. ASSUMPTION (not checked against the EGT documentation): `DPORTEE` is the straight line between origin and destination, close to zero for a loop. This change goes further than the French pipeline for the strolls that are loops (decision 1): it places the loop exactly at its start, so the loop leg, and a following trip back to the place the loop started from, have zero length.
- `W_ZWD` exists only for the interview modes that ask it (not the paper questionnaire, not children under 14). The stage's leisure subtype is drawn for every synthetic leisure leg from P(group | mode, travel-time band), estimated on the labelled MiD legs, and the leisure subtype distance layers are built from the MiD leisure legs; the donor's own `W_ZWD` is carried on the trips table but not read there. 710 and 711 are codes of the group `leisure_local`, so both estimates count the strolls and dog walks unless the loops are left out of them (decision 5); their share among the labelled walk leisure legs is the base rate in the survey manifest above (metric "chain rules that detect a loop on the trips that carry a detailed purpose").

## Decision

1. **Paris model for loops: a loop activity gets the place of the activity before it** (owner decision of 2026-11-25). A stroll is a loop unless it is out-and-back (owner decision of 2026-11-26): it starts at home and the person's next DIARY trip goes home (the definition, the diary way home and the invented home trip that does not count: the note, section "Definitions"). An out-and-back stroll is no loop: the solver places it like any leisure leg (subtype from the decider, distance from the pool, no bound from the diary's own length, as the owner decided on 2026-11-25) and its way home keeps its drawn length. The trip count, the purposes and the times of the day are unchanged; only places and distances change. The trip after a loop starts at that place and keeps its own mode, times and drawn distance. The loop probability cells (starts at home, way home follows) are read from the diary on both sides, the MiD legs and the synthetic trips, so the plan repair's invented home trip counts as a way home on neither side (before this change only the synthetic side counted it).
2. **Which legs are loops, and where the loop is put,** is defined only in `docs/codebase/notes/leisure-loops.md`.
   No person is excluded because of an escort link, a portal stay or a passive joint link (owner decision of 2026-11-25): those anchors and links are keyed by the activity index, so the collapse translates them to the collapsed numbering before the solve and restores every row afterwards. Exactly two kinds of loop stay on the solver, because collapsing them would break a mechanism that needs the activity: a loop whose own activity is anchored or linked (it is the key of the anchor or link, in practice a stroll a child takes along with an adult), and a loop that starts at an outside stay (the stay is a gate, a road crossing at the region boundary, and a leisure activity at a portal facility is unproven in MATSim).
3. **Switch** `secondary_loop_zero_displacement` (default and production value: the key comment in `configs/base_bs.yml`); off leaves every output as before (feature record, test named there).
4. **Comparisons leave loops out where both sides can:** the model side of the secondary distance comparison and the secondary OD pairs of the detour circuity calibration drop loop legs where the cache carries `loop_origin`, and the W_ZWD group reference gets a loop-free variant next to the committed one (data record `mid2023_w_zwd_group_reference_without_loops`), which applies the rule of decision 5.
5. **Loop-free leisure estimates** (owner decision of 2026-11-25). A second switch, `secondary_leisure_estimates_without_loops` (`config_keys.KEY_SECONDARY_LEISURE_ESTIMATES_WITHOUT_LOOPS`; default and production value: the key comment and `configs/base_bs.yml`), leaves the loop legs, as defined in the note (`purpose_subtype.drop_loop_legs`; an out-and-back stroll stays in, because the stage places it like any leisure leg), out of the two leisure estimates the remaining legs use: the leisure subtype distance layers of the distance distributions stage and the estimate of the leisure subtype decider. It requires `secondary_leisure_subtype_split` and `secondary_loop_zero_displacement` (both stages refuse to configure without them); both stages read the same key of one resolved config, so they cannot differ within a run. Off leaves both estimates exactly as before. The rule, the two places, the reports and what stays untouched are defined in the note.
6. **Mode rules for strolls** (owner decisions of 2026-11-26). Two eqasim-java-bs trip constraints keep a stroll trip on walk or bicycle where the mode choice evaluates it; the definitions, the exemptions and what each does not reach are in `docs/codebase/notes/leisure-loops.md`, section "Mode choice".
   - **Same-facility rule** `SameFacilityLeisureNonMotorised` (`org.eqasim.braunschweig.mode_choice.constraints.SameFacilityLeisureTripConstraint`). A loop activity sits at the facility of the activity before it, so the trip into it and the way home after it start and end at one facility, and a mode choice that is free to pick a car would produce a car trip between two identical places. The rule reads the place and needs no marker, so it does not depend on the loop switch.
   - **Stroll rule** `StrollNonMotorised` (`org.eqasim.braunschweig.mode_choice.constraints.StrollTripConstraint`). An out-and-back stroll has a destination of its own, so no place tells it apart: the rule reads the activity attribute `outAndBackStroll`, which the population writer sets from the column `is_out_and_back_stroll` of the secondary location stage (the path: the note, section "From the stage to the plans"). It covers the trip INTO the stroll only; the way home stays free (ASSUMPTION below). Loops carry no marker: a collapsed loop is covered by the same-facility rule, and a loop kept on the solver keeps today's free mode choice. Reason: the tour of an out-and-back stroll (home, stroll, home) never touches an `outside` stay, so the border tour rule of #678 never pins it, while a kept loop after an outside stay can carry a car mode that the border rule pins (design ruling of 2026-11-26; the Java side needs no coordination between the two rules). Without the loop switch no activity carries the marker and the rule is inert.
   Both are selected by one switch, `braunschweig.mode_choice.loop_trips_non_motorised` (default and production value: the key comment in `configs/base_bs.yml`; no new switch for the stroll rule). `matsim.simulation.prepare` selects both constraints (mechanics, report and switch-off behaviour: the same note, section "Mode choice"). Merge order: the Java change (TUBS-IVS/eqasim-java-bs#67) must be merged and its jar built before a production run with the switch on, because the stage refuses an older jar. The switch has its own feature record `loop_trips_non_motorised`. The effect with the rules on, and the share of caught trips the mode choice does not evaluate (measured from the written populations), are recorded in the run manifest [`docs/runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml`](../runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml) (issue #679; arm D for the same-facility rule; for both rules the metrics "arm E - both Java rules, their selection and their own counts" and "arm E against arm D - modes of the out-and-back stroll legs and their ways home after the initial mode choice and after iteration 5").

## Approaches considered

- **Zero displacement at the start place (chosen).** Keeps the diary's trip count and times; the loop kilometres disappear from the walk and bicycle kilometres.
- **Out and back at half the loop length, for every stroll.** Keeps the kilometres, but needs a return trip where the survey has none, which changes trip counts against the participation controls the synthesis holds. Withdrawn for the strolls without a diary way home.
- **Out and back for the strolls that the diary records out and back (chosen for that subset, owner decision of 2026-11-26).** A stroll from home whose next diary trip goes home keeps a destination placed by the solver like any leisure leg, and the diary's own way home stays: no return trip is invented, so the trip counts do not change. The length is drawn from the pool, not set to half the diary's loop length (decision 1). This replaces the first version of this change, which collapsed every stroll and made the way home after a stroll from home zero length; it keeps the diary kilometres of the subset (the survey probe measures how close the two lengths are).
- **Own leisure group with a near-zero distance layer.** Touches every enumeration of the group names, and the leg after the loop keeps a drawn distance that cannot be met when the loop sits between two identical places.
- **Drop the stroll as a trip.** Lowers the leisure participation the synthesis controls against the regional survey, where a stroll counts as a leisure trip.
- **Exclude the strolls from the mode choice.** Possible only per tour (the tour filter), which would freeze every trip of the tour; the trip constraint of decision 6 leaves the other trips of the tour free. Rejected.
- **Keep the diary mode for the same-facility leisure trips.** Design reasoning, not measured: the stock vehicle continuity rule (`VehicleTourConstraint` of the discrete mode choice contrib, selected as `VehicleContinuity` in `tourConstraints`) requires a vehicle mode's vehicle to be where the trip starts, so a loop leg pinned to `bicycle` would force the tour's other legs or leave no valid chain once the mode choice moved the person there by another mode; allowing walk or bicycle lets the choice pick a valid one. Rejected.
- **An explicit marker in the population** (an attribute on the activity written by the synthesis and read by the Java side). For a loop the place already carries the information (one facility at both ends), so no attribute is needed: rejected for loops. Chosen for the out-and-back strolls, whose place carries nothing (decision 6).
- **Leave the subtype decider and the distance layers as they were (the first version of this change).** The leisure legs that remain would then be assigned to `leisure_local` more often than a loop-free estimate gives and would draw their distance from a pool that also holds loop path lengths. Replaced by decision 5.

## Assumptions

- ASSUMPTION (owner decision of 2026-11-26, which replaces the pending question): a stroll that starts at home and is followed by its own diary way home is out-and-back, not a loop. The survey diary shows such pairs with about equal lengths (context); the model does not reproduce that equality, it draws the length of the stroll from the pool. A stroll followed only by the plan repair's invented home trip is a loop: the diary ended with it, and the invented trip is no evidence of a way home. A stroll that starts elsewhere than at home is a loop even when a way home follows.
- ASSUMPTION: the loop probability estimated on the legs whose detailed purpose was asked holds where it was not asked (paper questionnaire, children under 14). The survey cannot test it: the unasked legs are exactly the ones without the label.
- ASSUMPTION: the probability estimated on leisure legs (`W_ZWECK` 7) does not transfer to the coarse purpose 10 or to the child purposes, so no loop is imputed for them.
- Loops with a motorised main mode (a drive without destination) stay as before: their vehicle kilometres belong on the network.
- Which legs draw a loop value and the fallback for thin cells are defined in the note: every in-scope leg whose `W_ZWD` is no leisure group code draws.
- ASSUMPTION (ruling R14 of the design review): a leg with `W_ZWD` 503, 603 or 605 (a shop or errand detail code on a leisure leg, members of `purpose_subtype.LEISURE_SENTINELS`) draws a loop value like the other members of `purpose_subtype.LEISURE_SENTINELS` (the "not asked" codes) and 799 under the codeplan sentinels. It carries no leisure label, so the labelled legs cannot tell whether such a leg is more or less often a loop; the survey cannot test it. How many synthetic legs draw per code: the survey manifest above.
- ASSUMPTION (owner decision of 2026-11-26): a leisure trip that starts and ends at one facility has no displacement and is not a motorised trip. The same-facility rule of decision 6 reads the place, not the loop flag, so it also catches any same-facility leisure trip that is no loop; it keeps the trips non-motorised that the mode choice evaluates, not all of them.
- ASSUMPTION (owner decision of 2026-11-26): the trip into an out-and-back stroll is not a motorised trip, so the stroll rule allows walk and bicycle there; the way home after the stroll stays free (the same-mode share of the way home after strolls in the survey: the artifact of the metric "how strolls (710), dog walks (711) and active sport (704) are recorded, by mode" of [`docs/runs/i679-stroll-loops-survey-probe-2026-11-25.yml`](../runs/i679-stroll-loops-survey-probe-2026-11-25.yml), measured on the strolls followed by a way home, whether or not they start at home). The model does not test whether the way home may differ from the way out in the same measure.
- ASSUMPTION: a loop kept on the solver carries no marker, so its trip keeps today's free mode choice even where the person walked in the survey (decision 6 gives the reason); how many loops stay on the solver: the stage report block `loops`.
- ASSUMPTION: a bicycle leg with the stroll code 710 or the dog-walk code 711 (a ride without destination) is a loop, like the same code on foot.
- ASSUMPTION: the subtype of a leisure leg that is not a loop depends on its mode and travel-time band only, as in the decider before this change. It does not depend on the two further inputs of the loop draw (the activity before is home, the way home follows), so a non-loop leg is not assigned a subtype conditional on them. The survey table can show whether the mix differs by these inputs; this change does not test it.
- ASSUMPTION: loop legs are identified by code and mode on LABELLED legs, so the subtype estimate is the mix conditional on "not a labelled loop". The unlabelled legs the model draws as non-loops are assumed to follow the same mix; the survey cannot test it (the unasked legs are exactly the ones without the label, see the assumptions above).
- ASSUMPTION: a loop kept on the solver (its own activity is anchored or linked, or it starts at an outside stay) is placed and drawn like any other leisure leg, so it draws its subtype and its distance from the loop-free estimates, although it is a loop by the flag.

## Consequences

- **Two kinds of loop keep their place on the solver** (decision 2): a loop whose own activity is anchored or linked, and the first loop after an outside stay. They are placed by the solver as before and counted by reason; how large that share is: the stage report block `loops` of a run. Every other loop of a person with an escort link, a portal stay or a passive joint link is collapsed, and the stage raises where the translation of an anchor or link does not hold (the rule: the note).
- **Escort runs follow the diary as recorded.** The escort link assigns its consecutive runs before the collapse (ruling R17 of the design review), so "escort, stroll, escort" keeps two escort runs, each restarting at the first linked child (rank 0); the same diary with the stroll removed by hand would be one run, so a comparison of the two switch states is a comparison of distributions here too.
- **The remaining leisure legs are estimated without the loops** (decision 5): the subtype of a leisure leg is drawn from the labelled MiD legs without the loop legs, and the `leisure_local` distance layer is built without them. Counts of the legs left out are in two stage reports (the note). Not changed: the aggregate `leisure` distance layer, the `leisure_unspecified` layer, the other purposes, the loop probability estimate (it needs the labelled loops) and motorised loops, which stay in both estimates. The direction and size of the effect on the distances at 1 %: the run manifest [`docs/runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml`](../runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml).
- **The lost loop kilometres.** The walk and bicycle kilometres of the model fall by the loops' own share, and by the legs that follow a loop and lose their length with it: the trip after a loop starts at the loop's place, so a trip back to the place the loop started from has zero length. A way home after a stroll from home no longer belongs to these legs, because that stroll is out-and-back and keeps both legs; what remains are the invented home trip after a stroll from home that ended the diary (its trip back to home has zero length) and a later trip that goes back to the place a loop started from. The count and kilometres of the legs that lose their length: the metric "non-loop walk and bicycle legs that lose their length because they follow a loop" of [`docs/runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml`](../runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml) (arms A to D were measured with the first version of the rule, which also collapsed the diary's way home; for the rule as decided, the lengths of the out-and-back strolls, of their ways home and the zero-length walk and bicycle legs of arm E are in the metric "arm E against arm D - length of the out-and-back stroll legs, their ways home, and all walk and bicycle legs" of the same manifest). This is a known difference to survey totals, which count loop lengths. The published MiD W12 table that `scripts/validate_secondary_distances.py` compares with keeps loops and carries no `W_ZWD`, so on that table the survey side cannot drop them (the script states it), while the W_ZWD group reference has the loop-free variant.
- **Trips from home straight back to home (#375)** already have this representation: in `synthesis/population/spatial/secondary/problems.py::find_assignment_problems` a chain whose two ends are fixed purposes and that has no variable activity gets size 0 and is skipped, so the trip starts and ends at the household's one home location (`synthesis/population/spatial/locations.py`). This change adds the same for strolls.
- **Facilities.** A leisure activity at a home, work or education facility is new for MATSim; the facility check accepts it only for the written ids of that kind. RunPreparation and a short simulation accepted it end to end: the run manifest [`docs/runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml`](../runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml).
- **Loop legs go through the mode choice, restricted.** A loop leg has no length, but it goes through the mode choice like any trip; without the same-facility rule a part of the loop legs gets a motorised mode, and the run manifest [`docs/runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml`](../runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml) holds the counts for the same legs with the loop placement switch off and on (the mode rule is not part of these arms), before and after the simulation's iterations (metric "modes of the loop legs after the initial mode choice"). With the mode rule on, a same-facility leisure trip can only be walk or bicycle where the mode choice evaluates it (definition and reach: the note); the trip into an out-and-back stroll is restricted the same way by the stroll rule, which the arms A to D did not have; the share it does not reach is counted by the Java factory and recorded, with the modes of the caught trips, in the run manifest [`docs/runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml`](../runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml) (arm D; with the stroll rule: arm E, metric "arm E - both Java rules, their selection and their own counts").
- **Out-and-back strolls are ordinary solver legs.** They stay in the leisure estimates and in the placement problems (their distances and subtypes come from the pool). How many strolls are out-and-back and how many loops are followed only by the invented home trip: the stage report block `loops`; at 1 %: the run manifest [`docs/runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml`](../runs/i679-stroll-loops-ab-1pct-2026-11-25.yml), metrics "arm E against arm D - strolls by kind (stage report block `loops`)", "... - length of the out-and-back stroll legs, their ways home, and all walk and bicycle legs" and "... - legs left out of the two leisure estimates".
- The solver's random streams are consumed by fewer legs with the switch on, so two switch states differ as distributions, not leg by leg.
- The secondary location stage, `matsim.scenario.facilities`, the day-view location join (`braunschweig.synthesis.commute_day.spatial_locations_day`) and the plans writer wrapper (`braunschweig.matsim.scenario.population`) declare the loop key, so their stage hashes change once, with the switch off too (the config key set is part of the hash). With the switch off no column `is_out_and_back_stroll` and no attribute `outAndBackStroll` is written, so the plans carry nothing of the stroll rule. The same holds for `secondary_leisure_estimates_without_loops`, which the secondary location stage and the distance distributions stage declare unconditionally: the distance distributions stage and everything downstream recompute once.
